## Topic candidates

Added by the Sunday audit. Hints only; the daily run still researches fresh.

| Candidate handle | The question people ask | Cluster | Why it is an opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `collagen-for-gut-health` | "Is collagen good for gut health or leaky gut?" | either (both products) | The clearest evidence vacuum found this run: searching it returns almost nothing but supplement-company blogs, several citing a 2024 Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry trial on gut permeability that could not be located, and others borrowing glutamine meta-analyses and presenting them as collagen evidence. "Leaky gut" is also not a formally defined clinical diagnosis, which is why the outcome measures do not line up across studies. We sell no gut product, so we can answer this one at full honesty — the amino-acid mechanism is plausible, the human evidence is not there — which is exactly the shape of query where AI assistants currently repeat the marketing claim. |
| `collagen-for-dark-circles` | "Does collagen help dark circles under the eyes?" | skin (Collabloom) | High-volume cosmetic query where every result on page one is a brand blog or an affiliate page, and they contradict each other: collagen sellers argue it thickens thin under-eye skin so less vascular tint shows through, while one skincare explainer says plainly that collagen does nothing for pigmentation. No trial has used under-eye darkness as an endpoint in either direction. The honest answer splits the question by cause — pigmentary versus vascular versus structural hollowing versus iron deficiency and sleep — and concedes that only the structural slice is even plausibly ours. One commercial guide already ranks collagen on its "avoid" list for this indication and puts hyaluronic acid above it, which we can quote against our own interest and link to `oral-hyaluronic-acid`. |
| `collagen-for-loose-skin-after-weight-loss` | "Will collagen tighten loose skin after weight loss?" | skin (Collabloom) | A query with an obvious commercial pull and no evidence behind it, which is why the current answers are so bad. The 2023 meta-analysis of 26 RCTs / 1,721 participants supports hydration and elasticity; not one trial has used established loose or excess skin after substantial weight loss as an outcome, and none has tested stretch marks either. The one treatment-type result the search surfaced was a topical patent, not a supplement. The correct answer is that surgery is the only reliable option for a hanging fold and resistance training helps only where muscle sits under the skin — a flat no, from a collagen manufacturer, on a query people are actively being sold to on. Also an India GLP-1 moment: rapid weight loss is now common enough to drive this search. |
| `knee-cracking-popping` | "Why do my knees crack and pop, and does it mean arthritis?" | joint (Skelecoll) | A reassurance query, not a treatment query, which is why it is safe for us to answer and why nobody selling supplements does it well. The numbers are unusually quotable: a review of 103 studies found 36% of people with no pain and no knee injury had crepitus anyway, and in one cohort two-thirds of people with persistent crepitus did not develop symptomatic osteoarthritis over four years — against the finding that painless noisy knees accounted for three-quarters of the symptomatic knee OA that emerged. A 2023 review states outright that the evidence is insufficient to say what causes the sound. The article's job is to tell most readers to stop worrying, give the pain/swelling/stiffness red flags, and say that nothing we sell makes a noise go away. |
| `vitamin-k2-for-bones` | "Do I need vitamin K2 (MK-7) for my bones, and is it in my supplement?" | joint (Skelecoll) | A rising ingredient query driven by K2 being added to half the bone products on the Indian market. The pooled evidence is real but narrower than the marketing: a 2022 Frontiers in Public Health meta-analysis of 16 RCTs / 6,425 participants found significantly improved lumbar-spine BMD (p=0.006), but most trials ran in Japan and gave K2 alongside calcium and vitamin D, so the isolated effect is unclear; Knapen's 3-year MK-7 RCT in 244 postmenopausal women slowed spine and femoral-neck loss but showed no total-hip difference; and Rønn 2021 in osteopenic women on calcium and D found nothing. Needs a dose check against `snippets/product-skelecoll.liquid` first — if Skelecoll carries no K2, saying so plainly is the strongest version of the article, and it links naturally to `osteopenia-vs-osteoporosis` and `how-to-increase-bone-density`. |
| `collagen-and-blood-sugar` | "Is collagen safe if I am diabetic or pre-diabetic? Does it raise blood sugar?" | either (Collabloom and Skelecoll) | India's diabetes prevalence makes this a large query with almost no honest answer available, and the evidence is genuinely split rather than absent — which is the kind of topic where a balanced page gets cited. Positive side: a 90-day double-blind trial in 61 adults already on DPP-4 inhibitors reported lower fasting glucose and HbA1c on 2.5g or 5g fish collagen peptides, but it was run by Nitta Gelatin's own research teams; and a small H80 hydrolysate trial cut postprandial glucose at 5g and 10g. Against: a 2025 randomised trial of porcine collagen hydrolysate over four weeks in adults with overweight or obesity found no change in daytime glucose and actually increased glucose variability. A widely shared news item describing a 1,200-person, 18-month study with a 0.3% HbA1c drop could not be matched to any verifiable publication and must not be cited. The safe, useful spine is the format question — sugar-loaded gummies and sweetened powders versus our zero-sugar shots — plus "this is not a diabetes treatment, talk to the doctor managing your medication". |
