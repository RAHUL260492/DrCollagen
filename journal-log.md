| `best-time-to-take-collagen` | "When is the best time to take collagen — morning or night, with food or empty stomach?" | either (both products) | Verified by search on 2026-10-11: no trial has compared morning against evening, or fasted against fed. Every page currently ranking asserts an answer anyway, usually a seller citing hormone rhythms. What the trials actually controlled was daily consistency over 8 to 24 weeks. An honest "nobody has tested this" answer directly contradicts a thin consensus, which is exactly the shape of query where AI assistants currently repeat the marketing claim. |
| `is-collagen-vegetarian` | "Is collagen vegetarian? Is there a vegetarian or Jain collagen?" | skin (Collabloom, Dr Raashi Mehta) | Heavy Indian intent — vegetarian, Jain and halal all search this — and the current answers are either promotional or generic. Nobody states plainly that "plant-based collagen" products contain no collagen at all and are vitamin C, zinc and copper co-factor blends. Requires the inconvenient disclosure up front: Collabloom and Skelecoll are marine, so they are not vegetarian. Publishing that honestly is the only version worth writing. |
| `oral-hyaluronic-acid` | "Does oral hyaluronic acid actually work for skin, or only in a serum?" | skin (Collabloom, Dr Raashi Mehta) | Indian results conflate topical serums with swallowed hyaluronic acid and answer the wrong question. Oral HA has its own small trial literature around 120mg daily that almost nobody in India cites. Writable for both products: Skelecoll carries 120mg hyaluronic acid, Collabloom carries hyaluronic acid with 30mg oral ceramides — doses to be re-read from the product snippets at writing time. |
| `how-much-collagen-per-day` | "How much collagen should I take per day?" | either (both products) | Searched 2026-10-18: there is no official daily guideline, and the pages ranking give a flat "2.5 to 15g" range without explaining the one distinction that actually matters — hydrolysed collagen peptides are dosed in grams (2.5-5g for skin trials, 5-10g for joint trials) while undenatured type II collagen is dosed at 40mg, a 250-fold difference. Readers comparing a 40mg UC-II capsule against a 10g shot think the capsule is weaker or mislabelled. We can do the arithmetic on our own labels (Collabloom 8,000mg, Skelecoll 10,000mg — re-read at writing time) and say where each sits against the trial doses. Does not overlap `how-long-does-collagen-take-to-work`, which answers timeline, not quantity. |
| `osteopenia-vs-osteoporosis` | "My DEXA says osteopenia — is that the same as osteoporosis?" | joint (Skelecoll, Dr Nachiket Pansey) | High-anxiety query from people holding a scan report, and the India angle is real rather than decorative: the WHO bands (T-score -1.0 to -2.5 osteopenia, -2.5 or below osteoporosis) are applied through a reference database, and studies comparing South Asian Indian reference standards against US-white standards find they classify differently — so which database your scanner used changes your label. Also the chance to explain FRAX and why the T-score alone does not decide treatment. Supplements play an honestly small supporting role here, which suits us. Pairs with `how-to-increase-bone-density`. |
| `collagen-for-nails` | "Does collagen actually help brittle nails?" | skin (Collabloom, Dr Raashi Mehta) | Collabloom is sold for nails, so this needs an honest page rather than a marketing one. Nearly every claim in the category traces back to a single trial — 25 women, 2.5g daily for 24 weeks, nail growth +12% and broken nails -42%, published in J Cosmet Dermatol — which was open-label, single-centre, had no placebo arm and was run on a manufacturer's branded peptide, and whose own authors asked for larger placebo-controlled studies. Nobody ranking says any of that. Also lets us state that nails grow roughly 3mm a month, so 24 weeks is the minimum honest trial window. |
| `collagen-for-gut-health` | "Is collagen good for gut health or leaky gut?" | either (both products) | The clearest evidence vacuum found this run: searching it returns almost nothing but supplement-company blogs, several citing a 2024 Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry trial on gut permeability that could not be located, and others borrowing glutamine meta-analyses and presenting them as collagen evidence. "Leaky gut" is also not a formally defined clinical diagnosis, which is why the outcome measures do not line up across studies. We sell no gut product, so we can answer this one at full honesty — the amino-acid mechanism is plausible, the human evidence is not there — which is exactly the shape of query where AI assistants currently repeat the marketing claim. |
